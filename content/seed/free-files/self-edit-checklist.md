## Objective

A completed pre-publish check on a specific piece of writing — three steps for reviewing it with fresh eyes, and five rules for the prose itself.

## Intended user

Founders and small teams who write their own copy without an editor to catch what they can't see themselves.

## Situation

Before publishing or sending anything you've written, work through it in two stages. First, create distance: give it time, print it out, read it aloud. Then check it rule by rule against George Orwell's five writing rules — clichéd language, inflated vocabulary, unnecessary words, passive voice, jargon.

Use before publishing or sending any piece of writing you want to be at its best.

## Process

1. Give it time — set the draft aside for at least a day, then come back to it.
2. Print it out — mark it up with a pencil to create distance from your own words.
3. Read it aloud — listen for repetitions, clumsy phrases and dead rhythm.
4. Check for clichéd figures of speech — replace any phrase you're used to seeing in print with something said directly.
5. Check for inflated vocabulary — replace any long word with a short one that does the same job.
6. Check for unnecessary words — cut anything the sentence doesn't need.
7. Check for passive voice — rewrite it active wherever you can.
8. Check for jargon — replace any technical or foreign phrase with an everyday equivalent.

Expected to take roughly 5–10 minutes per piece.
