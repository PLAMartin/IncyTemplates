# Idea Intake

## Objective

A one-page record of the idea as it stood on day one, for later comparison — capture it before evaluation begins, while the reasoning is still fresh.

## Intended user

Founders starting the Idea Validation Pack.

## When to use

Use as the first step of the pack, before any evaluation begins.

## When not to use

Not needed if you've already completed the free Product Idea Snapshot for this idea.

## Required context

Have on hand: a one-sentence description of the idea, where it came from, and the reason it's being taken seriously right now (a customer conversation, a personal frustration, a competitor gap, etc.).

## Questions the AI agent must ask

- What is the idea, in one sentence?
- Where did it come from — a specific conversation, observation, or existing frustration?
- Why is this idea being taken seriously *now*, rather than dismissed like most ideas are?
- Who do you believe has this problem today?
- What would have to be true for this to be worth building?

## Information the agent may infer

The founder's rough confidence level and the idea's category (product, feature, business model) can be inferred from how the idea is described, but should be confirmed rather than assumed outright.

## Fields to complete

1. **Idea, one sentence** — the idea stated as plainly as possible.
2. **Source** — where it came from (conversation, observation, frustration, market gap).
3. **Why now** — the specific trigger that made this worth writing down today.
4. **Believed audience** — who you think has this problem, in one sentence.
5. **What would have to be true** — the one or two assumptions this idea depends on.
6. **Confidence today (1–5)** — a plain gut-check number, for comparison later.

## Completion criteria

All six fields above are filled in with specific, falsifiable statements rather than vague aspirations — "freelance designers who bill hourly" not "creative people."

## Interpretation guidance

This record is not a validation step — it's a dated snapshot. Its value comes later, when you compare what you believed on day one against what you've since learned through Customer Discovery.
