If you're going to complete every task on a list eventually, the order barely matters. Most task lists aren't like that — deadlines slip, some tasks matter far more than others, and there's rarely time for everything. The right order depends entirely on what you're actually optimising for, and most people never stop to name it.

## Four scheduling strategies

**Earliest Due Date**: complete whichever task is due soonest, regardless of how long it takes. This minimises how badly anything slips when deadlines are the thing that matters. **Moore's Algorithm**: when you can't hit every deadline, deliberately drop or delay your most time-consuming task first — this minimises the *number* of overdue tasks rather than how late any one of them is. **Shortest Processing Time**: do the quickest task first. This clears your list fastest, reduces the number of open tasks quickly, and sustains momentum by making the workload feel more manageable. **Weighted Processing Time**: rank tasks by value divided by duration, and do the highest value-per-time task first. This is the right default whenever tasks genuinely differ in importance, not just in size.

## A second lens: importance and urgency

Eisenhower's decision matrix sorts tasks by two questions instead of one: is it important, and is it urgent? Important and urgent: do it first. Important but not urgent: schedule it deliberately, or it never happens. Urgent but not important: delegate it if you can. Neither important nor urgent: stop doing it. This is a useful sense-check before applying any of the four scheduling strategies above — it's easy to let "urgent" quietly stand in for "important" when they're not the same thing at all.

## Using the process

The **Weighted Priority Matrix** template combines both lenses in one worksheet — sort your tasks by importance and urgency first, then apply value-per-duration scoring to whatever's left once you know what actually matters. The **Priority Scorer** Tool asks a few questions about your current task list and recommends which of the four scheduling strategies fits best right now, with a runner-up and the reason it won.

None of these strategies is universally correct — the right one depends on what's true about your list *today*. Revisit the question whenever the shape of what you're facing changes.
