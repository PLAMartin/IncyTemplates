Product Naming System gets you something to call the thing. Now someone has to actually use it — and "launch and hope people find it" isn't a plan, it's a wish. Finding your first customers takes the same deliberate approach as everything else in this journey: start from what you already know, commit to one thing, and get specific.

## Start warm, not cold

Your very first customers are almost never strangers. They're people who've already shown some signal — they mentioned the problem to you, they're in your existing network, they've engaged with something you've written on the topic. Warm leads convert at a completely different rate to cold ones, because you're not asking them to trust a stranger; you're following up on something real. Exhaust your warm list properly before you spend energy on cold outreach at all.

## Pick one channel and commit to it

The instinct when launching is to be everywhere at once — a bit of cold email, a bit of content, a post in a few communities, maybe some ads. In practice this means you do all of them badly and none of them long enough to know if they'd have worked. Pick the single channel that best matches where your specific audience already spends attention and your own strengths as a founder, and run it deliberately for long enough to get a real answer, before adding a second one.

## Make outreach specific enough that it couldn't be a template

The fastest way to get ignored is a message that could have been sent to anyone. Reference something real and specific about the person or their situation — a job change, a public post about the exact problem, a mutual connection. It takes longer per message, which is exactly why it's rare, which is exactly why it works. Volume is not the lever; specificity is.

## Track people, not just numbers

"We need 50 signups" is a target, not a plan. Keep a running list of actual named people you're pursuing, why you picked them, and what happened. A list of ten real names with real context produces a completely different kind of learning than a spreadsheet of vanity metrics — you'll notice patterns in who says yes, who says no, and why, far faster than a dashboard will show you.

## Using the process

The **First 10 Customers Plan** template turns this into a working document: your warm-lead list, the one channel you've committed to, an outreach script specific enough to actually work, and a running log of named prospects and what happened with each. The **Channel Selector** Tool scores a specific channel against your situation — how present your audience actually is there, how well it fits your own strengths, how much effort a first real result takes, and whether it's genuinely repeatable — and gives you a fit score and a next step, so the "which one channel" decision is evidence-led rather than a guess.

That closes the loop on the founder journey this platform covers: assess the idea, gather real evidence, decide what to do, scope what to build, name it, and find the people who'll actually use it.
