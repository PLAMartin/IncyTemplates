Most startup advice quietly assumes SaaS — build software, charge a monthly fee, done. It's a fine model, but it's one of several, and defaulting to it without checking the fit costs you later: the wrong business model makes growth harder, pricing more confusing, and every metric you track less useful. Established companies can combine models; a startup should get one right first.

## Two questions that actually separate the models

Underneath the jargon, most business models differ on two things: **who pays you**, and **how your product delivers value**. Software-as-a-Service is paid directly by the person using it, on an ongoing basis. A Marketplace is paid — usually a cut — by whichever side completes a transaction, and it only works because it connects two distinct groups who need each other. A Transactional business is paid a small fee every time a transaction happens, whether or not the two sides even know your name. An Advertising business is paid by someone who isn't the end user at all — the advertiser is the customer; the user is the product being reached.

## Four common models

**SaaS** provides ongoing access to a cloud-based service — think Zoom or Slack — paid monthly or annually, growing through self-service sign-up, direct sales, or both. **Marketplace** apps enable transactions between sellers and buyers, like Airbnb or eBay; the hard part is the cold-start problem — you need supply before you get demand, or demand before you get supply — and markets often end up with a single winner. **Transactional** businesses, often fintech and payments — Stripe, Coinbase — take a small cut (1–3% is typical) of a high volume of transactions; the fee is thin, but consistency at volume makes it work. **Advertising** businesses — Facebook, Twitter — give the product away free and monetise the audience by selling access to it; you need a large, engaged user base before advertisers will pay for it, and few players usually dominate a given attention market.

## Growth follows the model

Each model has a realistic growth lever, and it's worth being honest about which one you can actually pull. SaaS grows through self-service sign-up or direct sales. Marketplace grows through solving the cold-start problem and then riding network effects — more supply attracts more demand, and vice versa. Transactional grows by plugging into existing flows of transactions. Advertising only works once you've reached genuine audience scale — there's no shortcut around needing a lot of engaged users first.
