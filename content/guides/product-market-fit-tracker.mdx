First Customers Planner gets real people using the product. That's the point where most founders stop measuring and start assuming — a few sign-ups start to feel like proof. They aren't. Product-market fit has one reliable anchor signal and a handful of supporting ones, and checking them honestly, on a regular cadence, is a completely different exercise from watching a signup counter go up.

## Ask the one question that matters

Sean Ellis's test cuts through the noise: ask real users how they'd feel if they could no longer use the product — very disappointed, somewhat disappointed, or not disappointed. If a strong share genuinely say "very disappointed," you have a real signal. If they don't, no amount of traffic or funding fixes that; you have a product problem, not a growth problem. Ask it directly, to real users, and treat the answer as the anchor everything else gets checked against.

## Watch what people do, not just what they say

Retention is the behavioural version of the same question. People who found real value come back on their own, without being emailed, nudged or discounted back in. A product with a strong "very disappointed" score but a retention curve that falls to zero within weeks has a contradiction worth investigating — someone's answer and their behaviour don't agree, and behaviour is harder to fake than a survey response.

## Look for growth you didn't have to buy

Organic growth and referral are the clearest external evidence of fit: new users showing up without you personally chasing or paying for them, and existing users bringing others in unprompted. Paid acquisition can make a mediocre product look busy for a while. Nobody refers a product they don't genuinely rate.

## Check whether anyone will actually pay

Willingness to pay — real payment, or a clear and specific commitment to pay — is the sharpest filter of all, because it costs the person something to say yes. "I'd probably pay for that" in conversation is cheap; a card number at checkout is not. If people are already paying, or have made a specific, costed commitment to, treat that as strong evidence; anything softer than that is still just an opinion.

The **PMF Signal Tracker** template gives you a survey script for the Sean Ellis question and a running log for the four supporting signals, so you're building a real record over time rather than relying on memory. This isn't a one-off gate you pass and move on from. Product-market fit shifts as the product, the market and your users change, so the honest answer is to run this check again on a regular cadence — not just once, right after launch.
