MVP Scoper gets you a scope to build. At some point that thing needs a name — and naming decisions have a specific failure mode: everyone in the room has an opinion, nobody has criteria, and the name that wins is usually whichever one the most senior or most persistent person in the room liked. That's not a naming process, it's a popularity contest with extra steps.

## Check availability before you fall in love with anything

This is the step founders skip, and it's the one that costs the most later. Before you get attached to a name, do a rough check: is the domain available, are the social handles free, does an informal trademark search turn up an obvious conflict in your category? A name that's taken everywhere isn't a worse option than the others — it's not an option at all, however memorable it is. Screen candidates for availability early, not after you've already started designing a logo around your favourite.

## Score what's left on memorability, clarity and distinctiveness

Once you have a shortlist of genuinely available names, three criteria do most of the work:

**Memorability** — can someone hear it once and repeat it back correctly later? Short, pronounceable, and unusual-but-not-alien names tend to win here.

**Clarity** — does the name hint at what the product actually does, or at least not actively mislead? Total literalism ("InvoiceApp") is boring but safe; total abstraction is memorable but risky, because you're paying an education cost every time you introduce the product.

**Distinctiveness** — does it stand out from the other names already in your category, or does it blend into a wall of similar-sounding competitors? A name that's easily confused with an established competitor's is a name you'll spend years correcting people about.

## Don't let one strong criterion carry a weak name

A name that's extremely memorable but completely unclear, or extremely clear but forgettable, usually isn't actually a strong name — it's a name that's strong on one axis and hoping nobody notices the others. Score candidates across all three criteria rather than picking whichever one has the single most impressive story attached to it ("it means X in Latin!").

## Test the survivors with real people, not just yourselves

Once you're down to two or three genuinely available, well-scoring names, the room's opinion stops being useful — you're too close to the product to hear the name the way a stranger will. Say each candidate out loud to a handful of people outside the project and see which ones they can repeat back, spell correctly, and guess the meaning of. That's a better signal than another internal debate.
