Habits aren't built by one great feature — they're built by a loop that reliably pulls someone back. Nir Eyal's Hook Model describes that loop in four stages, and most products don't fail because every stage is weak. They fail because one specific stage is weak, and no amount of polish on the other three fixes it.

## The four-stage loop

**Trigger** prompts the user to act — an external trigger (a notification, an icon, an email) at first, and eventually an internal one (a feeling, a habit, an association) once the loop has run enough times. **Action** is the behaviour taken in expectation of a reward — and for a behaviour to actually happen, three things need to be present at once: motivation, ability and a trigger (B=MAT). The simpler the action, the more reliably it happens. **Reward** is the payoff — and it works best when it's variable rather than predictable, appealing to something tribal (social recognition), hunter-like (resources, information) or self-oriented (mastery, achievement). **Investment** is what the user puts in — effort, data, content, reputation — which both improves the product for them and loads the trigger for next time.

## Get people to value quickly

The Bowling Alley Framework tackles a related problem: getting a new user to their first real payoff before they give up. Build a straight line to value by stripping every unnecessary step out of onboarding — if a step isn't required to reach the first meaningful outcome, cut it. Use product bumpers (a personalised welcome, a short tour, a progress bar, a checklist) to nudge people back on track inside the product, and conversational bumpers (onboarding emails, notifications, explainer videos) to bring them back when they've drifted away entirely.

## Find your weakest link

Most engagement problems trace back to exactly one stage of the loop, not all four. A weak trigger means people forget you exist. A hard action means they mean to, but don't. A flat reward means the habit never forms. No investment means every session starts from zero. Diagnosing which one is actually holding you back matters more than optimising a stage that was never the problem.

## Using the process

The **Engagement Loop Canvas** template walks through mapping your own product's trigger, action, reward and investment in writing. The **Engagement Loop Mapper** Tool asks about the strength of each stage and tells you which one is weakest — the one worth fixing first.

This isn't a one-off diagnosis. Revisit it as your product changes; the weakest link today won't necessarily be the weakest link once you've fixed it.
