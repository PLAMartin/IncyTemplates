Your brain has a bouncer. The limbic system filters almost everything you encounter down to a handful of things worth paying attention to — which means most of what you say about your product gets filtered out before a potential customer even consciously registers it. Positioning isn't about being clever; it's about getting past that bouncer and then giving people a reason to remember you once they're in.

## What a strong positioning statement actually does

A strong brand is a deliberate pairing: an action, taken with your product, that reaches a desired outcome — and, ideally, ties back to something your ideal customer admires or wants to be associated with. BlackBerry didn't market itself as "a phone for staying in touch"; it paired the action of checking email with the outcome of business productivity, and built a brand around people who saw themselves as serious and connected. The pairing is what makes a positioning statement stick, not the adjectives around it.

## Five ways to get past the brain's bouncer

Once you know what you're saying, you still have to get it noticed. Five things reliably do: **scary** (the problem you solve is something people already fear — fear is a powerful, primal filter-breaker); **strange** (something unusual or unexpected demands a second look); **sexy** (visually or emotionally striking presentation grabs attention on a primal level); **free gifts** (something genuinely valuable, well packaged, given away before you ask for anything); and **familiar** (repetition builds trust — it takes roughly eleven positive interactions before a brand really sticks, so plan for many small touchpoints, not one big pitch). Pick whichever one is actually realistic for your product and situation — trying to be all five at once usually means being memorably none of them.
