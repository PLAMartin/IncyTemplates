Nobody remembers when Facebook, Twitter or TikTok launched. Their launches weren't a big deal — and yours doesn't need to be either. If you're not a little embarrassed by the first version of what you're launching, you've probably launched too late. There's no limit to how many times you can do it, so the goal isn't to get one perfect launch right — it's to launch early, learn, and launch again.

## Four places to launch

**Soft launch page**: a short description and a register-interest button, before you even have a product to share — the lowest-risk way to test whether the idea alone generates interest. **Friends and family**: share with people you trust and watch how they actually interact with it, not just what they say — useful, though the feedback will be biased, so treat it as a signal rather than a verdict. **Community or social**: post where your audience already gathers — a relevant online community (each has its own rules and culture, so understand them first) or your own social channels — in a conversational way, focused on the value you offer. **Press**: a release and outreach to outlets, once you actually have something newsworthy to say — don't lead with press before you have a real story to tell.

## Pick a starting point, not a finish line

These four aren't a checklist to complete in order on a fixed schedule — they're an escalation to move through as your product and your confidence grow. Where you start depends on what you actually have today: nothing built yet favours the soft launch page; something built but untested favours friends and family first; an existing audience favours going straight to community or social; a genuinely newsworthy angle is worth leading with press.
